Capital

Your portfolio appreciates. The margin behind it earns nothing.

Pledging turns idle collateral into interest-free trading margin without selling a single share. That is genuinely useful, and it is also how people lose portfolios they never intended to touch.

By Anuj Baid Updated 18 August 2026 9 min read
Short answer

Pledged margin is trading margin your broker releases against shares you already own and continue to own. Because it is collateral rather than a loan, there is no interest. The broker applies a haircut, so roughly ₹75–85 lakh of usable margin against ₹1 crore of liquid holdings is a reasonable expectation. That margin can fund a systematic options strategy, so one pool of capital produces two return streams. The cost is that trading losses are settled against your collateral, and a bad enough loss can force the sale of the very holdings you pledged in order to avoid selling.

01The idle-collateral problem

Nobody buys a flat and leaves it empty. It appreciates and it earns rent. A long-term equity portfolio, by contrast, does exactly one job: it appreciates. The capital sitting inside it does nothing else for the entire holding period.

That is not obviously wrong — appreciation is why you bought it. But the collateral value of those holdings is a separate, unused asset. Your broker will lend trading margin against it at no interest, and most portfolios never make use of that.

02How pledging works

Mechanically it is a depository instruction, not a transaction:

  • You select holdings from your demat account and submit a pledge request through your broker.
  • The depository (NSDL or CDSL) sends you an authorisation link, typically confirmed with an OTP against your registered contact details.
  • Once confirmed, the securities are marked as pledged in favour of the broker. They remain in your demat account.
  • The broker values them, applies the prescribed haircut, and credits the balance as collateral margin available for derivatives positions.

Unpledging reverses this, usually with a settlement-cycle delay before the holdings are free to sell again. There is generally a small per-instruction fee in each direction.

03What you keep

Because nothing is sold, the things people worry about losing are not affected:

OwnershipUnchanged; shares stay in your demat account
DividendsContinue to be credited to you
Bonus and split entitlementsAccrue to you as normal
Capital-gains holding periodUnbroken; no sale event occurs
Price appreciationEntirely yours
Ability to sell instantlyLost until you unpledge

That last row is the real trade. Pledged holdings are not immediately sellable, which matters if your equity portfolio doubles as your emergency liquidity.

04The haircut

The broker does not credit the full market value. It applies a haircut to protect itself against the collateral falling in value between the moment a loss occurs and the moment it can be liquidated.

Haircuts vary by instrument. Liquid large caps and index ETFs sit at the favourable end; volatile or thinly traded stocks at the other, and some instruments are not accepted as collateral at all. Brokers publish an approved list with the applicable haircut, and both the list and the percentages are revised periodically — a stock that qualifies today may not next quarter.

05The cash-collateral requirement

One detail that catches people out: you generally cannot run a derivatives book on pledged collateral alone. Exchanges require a portion of the margin to be met in actual cash, with the remainder allowed as collateral. The split is commonly around half and half.

Practically, this means pledging ₹1 crore does not give you ₹80 lakh of freely deployable margin with no cash on hand. You need a cash component alongside it, and if your strategy's losses eat into that cash, the broker may charge interest on the shortfall until it is replenished. Budget for this before sizing anything.

06Funding a second engine

Once released, that margin can fund a systematic options strategy that is entirely independent of the equity portfolio. The portfolio keeps compounding at whatever the market delivers; the margin behind it runs a separate return stream. One pool of capital, two engines.

The strategies that fit this role well share a particular profile: defined risk, hedged rather than naked, and sized against a drawdown ceiling rather than against available margin. A strategy that can lose an unbounded amount is a poor match for collateral you cannot afford to lose — which is most of the point of running hedged index option structures rather than naked ones.

Sizing discipline

The available margin is not the correct position size. The correct position size is whatever keeps the worst plausible drawdown inside a limit you set in advance and can actually tolerate. Those two numbers are usually far apart, and the gap between them is the safety margin.

07The risk, stated plainly

Read this twice

Losses on the derivatives position are settled against your collateral. If the strategy loses enough, your pledged holdings can be sold to cover the shortfall. The portfolio you pledged specifically because you did not want to sell it is exactly what is at risk.

Three aggravating factors deserve attention:

  • Correlation. A sharp market fall reduces the value of your collateral at the same moment a short-volatility strategy is most likely to be losing and margin requirements are most likely to be raised. All three move against you together.
  • Forced timing. Liquidation to meet a margin call happens on the broker's schedule, not yours — typically at the worst available prices.
  • Loss beyond capital. Short option positions can lose more than the margin allocated to them. Pledging does not cap your downside at the pledged amount.

This is why the honest framing is not "free money from idle assets." It is a deliberate decision to take on a second, correlated risk in exchange for a second return stream, using capital you had already committed to the market.

08Who this does not suit

  • Anyone whose equity portfolio is also their emergency fund. Pledged holdings are not quickly sellable.
  • Anyone who would be forced to change their long-term plan if the pledged portion were liquidated at a bad moment.
  • Anyone who cannot maintain the cash-collateral component comfortably alongside the pledge.
  • Anyone attracted specifically because the margin is interest-free. Zero borrowing cost does not make the position low-risk; it only makes it cheap to hold.

09Questions people ask

What is pledged margin?

Trading margin a broker releases against securities you already own, after you formally pledge them as collateral. You keep ownership, dividends continue, and your capital-gains holding period is unbroken, because nothing is sold. The broker haircuts the market value and credits the remainder as usable margin for derivatives.

Does pledged margin cost interest?

No. It is a collateral arrangement, not a loan, so no interest accrues on the released margin. Brokers charge a small per-instruction pledge and unpledge fee, and there may be charges if your cash-collateral component falls short, but there is no borrowing cost of the kind a loan against shares carries.

How much margin do you get after the haircut?

Roughly ₹75–85 lakh of usable margin against ₹1 crore of liquid holdings, as a general guide. Liquid large caps and index ETFs attract smaller haircuts; volatile or illiquid stocks attract larger ones, and some are not accepted at all. Your broker publishes the approved list and applicable haircuts, and both change.

What is the risk of trading on pledged margin?

Losses are settled against your collateral, so a large enough loss can force the sale of the holdings you pledged. A falling market makes this worse: collateral value drops while margin requirements typically rise and short-volatility strategies are losing. Short options can also lose more than the margin allocated to them.

Can I still sell pledged shares?

Not immediately. You must unpledge first, which takes a settlement cycle. If instant liquidity matters to you, pledge only the portion you are certain you will not need to touch.
